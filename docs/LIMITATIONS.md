# Evidence, scope and next steps

Every result in this repository comes from public recordings tested on held-out data. This page states what that
evidence establishes, where its edges are, and how each edge is closed in the plan.

## What the prototype demonstrates
- Breathing rate from commodity Wi-Fi CSI, evaluated against a reference respiration sensor (WiRe-Breath): 0.40 breaths/min mean error, 96.2% within ±2.
- Activity recognition and fall detection (SenseFi ResNet-18 architecture, UT-HAR), evaluated on whole recordings the model never saw: 96.0% accuracy, 16 of 16 falls caught with 1 false alarm in 96 recordings.
- Abnormal-motion scoring relative to each person's own running baseline.
- Rest during each crew member's sleep period (the challenge's "daily sleep hours"), from breathing and movement in the CSI: 97.9% scored as rest, none wrongly as awake, on still recordings.
- A rule-based, auditable alerting layer (debounce, confidence gating, context-aware limits, evidence for every alert). Simple rules decide when an alert is raised.

## Scope by design
- Atheera reports breathing, activity, rest and falls. Heart rate, sleep stages, sleep apnoea and seizure detection are research directions we want to explore; they are not part of the current system and are not claimed.
- It is decision support for a human (flight surgeon or crew) and is not a medical device. Heart rate, blood pressure, blood sugar, temperature and mood stay with dedicated instruments.

## Scope of the evidence, and what comes next

- **Earth recordings.** All recordings come from terrestrial labs, and no CSI dataset from orbit exists yet. Microgravity changes posture, multipath inside metal modules and how people move. *Next:* recordings in a metal-walled mock-up, then a live ESP32 or Intel 5300 stream (via CSIKit).
- **One data source per crew member.** Breathing and activity come from different public datasets, hardware, rooms and people. The demo assigns one source per crew member and does not claim fused sensing of one person from both. *Next:* record both signals together in the same room.
- **One person per sensing zone.** Neither dataset covers several people in one zone. Crew quarters are single-occupancy, which suits the use case. *Next:* separating several people in shared modules.
- **Replay, not live capture.** The dashboard replays recorded data in real time through the full pipeline. The live path is planned and not yet built.
- **A stricter benchmark than the published one.** UT-HAR's published 98% is leaky: its windows are overlapping slices of 556 continuous recordings, and 499 of its 500 test windows come from recordings that also supply training windows. With whole recordings held out the same recipe scores 91.9%. Our robust model (augmented for noise, receiver-gain changes, dead subcarriers and packet loss) scores 95.2%, and an ensemble of it with three GPU-trained seeds scores 96.0%; it caught all 16 held-out fall recordings with 1 false alarm in 96. The same people and room still appear in every split (UT-HAR has no subject IDs), so a new room or person will score lower. *Next:* fresh recordings of new people and rooms.
- **Breathing measured on short, still recordings.** On 20 s windows of WiRe-Breath the average error is 0.40 breaths/min (96% within ±2; quiet room 0.17, with a moving person nearby 0.63). The recordings are 30 s long, from 5 people lying or sitting still in two rooms. The rate itself has no trained parameters, so there is no train/test leakage; only the confidence calibration is fitted, and its reported numbers are cross-fitted by subject. *Next:* longer nights, more people and real crew quarters.
- **Method choices were made on the scored data.** Figures are therefore likely a little optimistic. *Next:* re-measure on fresh recordings before quoting final numbers.
- **Earlier numbers (4-5 breaths/min error) came from data bugs, not modelling.** The loader blended packets with random phases when resampling (amplitude about 20% low), and the pipeline chose among 234 columns of which only 52 carry a channel. Both are fixed and covered by tests.
- **Slow breathing near 6-7 breaths/min can be read at twice its rate.** The band-pass filter weakens the fundamental there and its 2nd harmonic can win. A second, wider-band estimate flags these readings as ambiguous and their confidence is set to 0, so they show as "unclear", never as a confident normal rate. On WiRe-Breath slowed to 0.6x, confident readings more than 3 breaths/min off fell from 13% to 4%. Below 6 breaths/min is outside the analysis band (0.1–0.6 Hz) by design and reads as "no reliable signal", which the fusion layer reports as degraded sensing, not as a measured rate.
- **The "breathing slows" event uses time-stretched real data.** No public dataset records genuinely slow breathing with Wi-Fi CSI, so FE1's event replays real recordings of the same person slowed to 0.7x (about 9.3 to 6.5 breaths/min), with the chest-belt reference stretched identically. *Next:* paced-breathing recordings.
- **No rate prior, by choice.** Every learned shortcut that pulled readings toward typical rates (10-18 breaths/min) was rejected, because it would hide the abnormal breathing the monitor exists to catch. Tests check that clean breathing at 7-34 breaths/min is reported where it is. The data contains few windows below 9.5 or above 22 breaths/min, so accuracy at those rates is less well established.
- **Rest, not sleep.** The tracker counts time with steady breathing and no body movement for 30 s. Wi-Fi cannot tell light from deep sleep, and someone lying still awake counts as resting (worn actigraphy has the same limit). On still WiRe-Breath recordings 97.9% is scored as rest and none as awake (`results/eval_rest.json`). The reverse error (awake scored as rest) needs a dataset that pairs breathing with labelled wake periods, and none is public. The sleep period itself comes from the crew schedule. *Next:* compare with a sleep diary or wrist actigraphy.
- **"Movement nearby" is a rule, not a trained detector.** Motion during which a resting crew member's own breathing stays steadily tracked is attributed to someone or something nearby (advisory, does not interrupt rest). If the breathing track breaks while the motion lasts, it escalates to the abnormal-motion warning. It is checked on WiRe-Breath recordings with a second person moving nearby. The case of a crew member moving while their breathing still reads steadily is not yet testable, because no dataset labels a sleeper's own movements together with breathing.
- **Receiver gain steps are handled.** Commodity Wi-Fi cards change their gain in steps. The motion measure for breathing tracks divides out each packet's overall gain; before that, gain steps read as 3 s bursts of body motion.
- **Synthetic placeholders.** Any track whose id starts with `syn_` is synthetic and labelled as such in the dashboard. It is only a fallback so the demo runs before the datasets are downloaded. With the datasets present, no synthetic track plays.
